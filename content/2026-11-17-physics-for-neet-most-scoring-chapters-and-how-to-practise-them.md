Physics is 45 questions and 180 marks of NEET, and it is where most students lose their rank. Biology rewards reading, but Physics rewards practice. You can't memorise your way through it. Here are the 12 chapters that give the best return on your time, and how to practise each one. No fluff.

*A note before you start: chapter weightage shifts a little every year. These picks come from long-running question trends, not a guarantee. Always cross-check with the latest NMC/NTA syllabus.*

## The 12 Chapters

**1. Current Electricity** — The most reliable scorer. Questions are formula-driven and predictable: Ohm's law, resistor combinations, Kirchhoff's laws, Wheatstone bridge, potentiometer, meter bridge. Practise by drawing the circuit and simplifying it step by step before touching any formula.

**2. Electrostatics** — Coulomb's law, electric field, potential, Gauss's law, capacitors. Many questions are direct once you know the formulas. Practise capacitor combinations and field-versus-potential relationships until they're automatic.

**3. Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism** — Biot-Savart law, Ampere's law, force on a moving charge, torque on a loop, galvanometer conversions. Learn the right-hand rules properly, because direction-based questions are free marks once you do.

**4. Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents** — Faraday's law, Lenz's law, self and mutual induction, LCR circuits, resonance, transformers. Short and formula-heavy. Practise the LCR phasor diagram until you can draw it from memory.

**5. Ray Optics and Wave Optics** — Mirror and lens formulas, refraction, prisms, total internal reflection, optical instruments, Young's double slit, diffraction, polarisation. Practise sign convention relentlessly, since most errors here are sign errors.

**6. Dual Nature of Matter, Atoms and Nuclei** — De Broglie wavelength, photoelectric effect, Bohr model, radioactivity, binding energy. Mostly formula plug-in questions, which makes this one of the easiest sections in the paper. Don't skip it.

**7. Electronic Devices (Semiconductors)** — Logic gates, p-n junction, diodes, Zener, transistors. A small chapter with fairly consistent questions. Practise truth tables and gate combinations.

**8. Thermodynamics and Kinetic Theory** — Laws of thermodynamics, PV diagrams, work done, heat engines, Carnot efficiency, degrees of freedom. Practise reading PV diagrams: area under the curve is work.

**9. Laws of Motion, Work, Energy and Power** — The foundation of mechanics. Free body diagrams, friction, pulley systems, conservation of energy, collisions. Practise drawing a free body diagram for every problem, even easy ones.

**10. Rotational Motion** — Moment of inertia, torque, angular momentum, rolling motion. This is where many students struggle and where good preparation pays off. Memorise standard moment of inertia values and the parallel and perpendicular axis theorems.

**11. Gravitation, Oscillations and Waves** — Orbital velocity, escape velocity, satellites, SHM, spring-mass systems, sound waves, Doppler effect, standing waves. Practise how time period and energy change with parameters.

**12. Properties of Matter (Fluids, Elasticity, Thermal Properties)** — Bernoulli's equation, viscosity, surface tension, Young's modulus, calorimetry. Quick to learn and quick to score. Practise units and dimension checks here.

## How to Practise

- **Concept first, formula second.** Read the NCERT chapter, then derive the formula once so you know where it comes from.
- **Solve, don't read.** Do 20 to 30 MCQs per chapter right after finishing it.
- **Do previous year questions by chapter.** PYQs show you how NEET actually asks things.
- **Keep an error notebook.** Write every mistake with the reason (concept gap, calculation slip, misread question). Revise this notebook, not the whole book.
- **Time yourself.** Aim for about 1 minute per Physics question in mocks.

## The One Rule That Matters

Master five chapters before you touch the next five. Starting with Current Electricity, Electrostatics, Laws of Motion, Optics and Modern Physics gives the fastest rise in your score. The gap between students who score 150 or more in Physics and those who stay under 100 is not talent. It is solved questions. Start with those five chapters and everything else builds on them.
